Objective
Part B assesses whether you can move from reading a classical machine learning paper selected
in Part A to actually building, running, and critically interrogating it. Unlike Part A, which
evaluated your ability to select and disclose, Part B evaluates the depth of your technical
engagement. You will be evaluated across three interlocking dimensions:
•Reasonable reproductionof the core contribution from your selected paper in Part A
•Experimental analysisthrough meaningful baselines and ablations of the selected paper
•Critical reflectionon where and why the method fails
Part B has three sections, each testing a different layer of engagement with your paper:
whether you understood it, whether you can implement it, and whether you can probe its limits.
The 6 to 7-day window gives you flexibility, but the total scope is designed for approximately 4
to 8 hours of focused work.

6Question 3: Ablation Study (35 marks)
Estimated Time:Approximately 1.5 to 2 hours
Question 3 asks you to probe the method: what happens when you remove a key component,
and where does the method break? These tasks require running real experiments on real data
and writing interpretations that go beyond describing the output.
Task 3.1 Two-Component Ablation (20 marks)
Remove or simplifyexactly two distinct componentsof the method proposed in your se-
lected paper from Part A, one at a time. Each ablation must beindependent: when testing
one component, keep all other parts of the method at their full settings. For each of the two
components, your notebook for this task must contain:
•Amarkdown cellnaming the component being ablated and explaining the role it plays
in the full method (2–3 sentences). The explanation must be specific to your paper.
•Codethat implements the ablated version of the method and records performance using
thesame evaluation metricused in Question 2.
•Aplot or tablecomparing the performance of the full method and the ablated version.
Save the visualisation to:
partB/results/
•Aparagraph (5–7 sentences)interpreting the result. Discuss whether removing the
component affected performance, whether the size of the change matches your expectation,
and what this reveals about the contribution of that component.
Important Note
Marking breakdown:
•Each component ablation is worth10 marks.
•4 marksfor the experimental setup and result.
•6 marksfor the interpretation and reasoning.
Task 3.2 Failure Mode (15 marks)
Construct or identifyone scenarioin which your method fails noticeably. This may be a
specific data distribution, a hyperparameter regime, or a structural condition under which the
method produces clearly worse output than expected. Your submission must contain:
•Amarkdown celldescribing the failure scenario and explaining why you expected the
method to struggle in that situation.
•Codethat demonstrates the failure, along with a plot or metric comparison saved to:
partB/results/
•Aparagraph (5–7 sentences)explaining why the method fails in this scenario. Your
explanation should connect the failure to aspecific assumption or design choice
identified earlier in Question 1.
7•One sentencesuggesting a concrete modification to the method that could address this
failure. No implementation is required.
Important Note
•Good failure modes are rooted in the method’s assumptions from Task 1.2.
•If one of your Task 1.2 assumptions is ’the data is linearly separable in kernel
space’, your failure mode might be a dataset where no standard kernel achieves
separability. The best failures close a loop with your understanding from Question
1.
•Task 3.2 is worth 15 marks: 5 marks for the empirical demonstration, 7 marks for
the explanation linking the failure to the method’s assumptions, and 3 marks for
the suggested modification.
